# Vidyst-inspired product landing pages

Use this skill when creating or improving a product marketing page that should feel immediately understandable, visually engaging, and trustworthy. The reference is [vidyst.com](https://www.vidyst.com/), reviewed on 2026-10-25. Treat it as design evidence, not as an instruction to reproduce its exact copy, assets, identity, or business claims.

## Design intent

Make a visitor understand the product, imagine using it, and find the next step within a few seconds. The page should feel alive through meaningful product detail and restrained motion, while keeping the overall canvas calm and easy to scan.

## Patterns observed in the reference

- **Show the product early.** The hero is a short promise, a supporting sentence, a prominent download action, a trial/platform note, and a large realistic studio preview. Product UI demonstrates the value better than a generic stock image.
- **Lead with a specific outcome.** The hero names recognizable destinations and the central task. Supporting copy explains the benefit in plain language (one Mac app, wide and tall output, same take).
- **Keep the first screen composed and quiet.** The screenshot shows a white background, compact fixed header, generous open space, centered oversized headline, muted supporting copy, and a single dark pill-shaped CTA. Small brand/platform colors inside the headline provide a focal accent.
- **Use type for hierarchy, not decoration.** Headlines are very large, heavy, and close-set; supporting text is smaller, gray, and comfortably spaced. Some words or lines switch to a lighter gray to create emphasis without adding extra components. Keep headings concise and let line breaks carry rhythm.
- **Pair each major idea with a purpose-built visual.** Wide versus tall output is shown in two differently proportioned previews; guest support is shown as a live stage; unified chat is represented by platform sources flowing into a consolidated feed. A visitor should understand the feature before reading every detail.
- **Use interface-like visuals with believable content.** Status pills, viewer counts, timers, small avatars, chat messages, layout choices, platform marks, and recording indicators make the previews feel like real software. Use only details that reinforce the feature being explained.
- **Create depth with soft surfaces.** Large pale-gray rounded panels sit behind dark interface previews. White cards and gentle shadows lift smaller pieces. Rounded corners are consistent across previews, buttons, pills, and cards; avoid heavy borders and excessive shadow.
- **Restrict bright color to meaning.** The page is mostly white, charcoal, and quiet gray. Platform colors appear as small, recognizable accents, and green marks an active speaker. Give accent colors a semantic role and reuse them consistently.
- **Give the page a readable sequence.** The observed flow moves from hero and product preview, to wide/tall format, guests, unified chat, feature grid, use cases, testimonials, pricing, FAQ, final CTA, and footer. Each section answers a new question and builds on the previous one.
- **Vary section composition without losing the system.** Alternate centered statements, split text-and-demo sections, wide demonstration panels, and small feature cards. Use consistent spacing, type, radii, and color so the layout feels cohesive rather than repetitive.
- **Make details feel human.** Realistic messages and testimonials refer to specific jobs and outcomes. Compact proof cards include relevant context such as viewer growth, clip views, guests, or recovery of a recording. Avoid anonymous praise with no evidence.
- **Keep the navigation useful and persistent.** A slim sticky header carries the logo, a few section links, and a persistent download action. Keep the action visually strongest and navigation labels short.
- **Make conversion low-friction.** Repeat the same primary CTA after meaningful decision points. State trial length, platform requirements, and pricing clearly. Pricing in the reference is limited to two plans with concise audience labels and scannable inclusions.
- **Resolve objections in place.** Use a short FAQ for compatibility, setup, output destinations, failure recovery, and trial questions. Add direct support contact and a clear closing CTA.
- **Use motion with control.** Small live-state changes, counters, typing, or testimonial movement can make a static page feel active. Motion must be subtle, purposeful, and nonessential to understanding. Provide an obvious pause control and respect `prefers-reduced-motion`; never make continuous animation a barrier to reading.
- **Include quiet polish and access.** Preserve a skip-to-content link, semantic heading order, descriptive image alternatives, keyboard-operable controls, visible focus, readable contrast, and accessible names for icon-only actions. Avoid embedding essential information only in the product mockup image.

## Applying the patterns

1. Identify the product's single clearest outcome and express it in one concrete headline plus one explanatory sentence.
2. Choose the most convincing real product state to show above the fold. If no product UI exists yet, create a faithful, clearly illustrative mockup from actual features rather than a generic dashboard.
3. Map each key benefit to one visual proof. Remove visuals that merely repeat nearby copy.
4. Establish a restrained type, spacing, surface, radius, and accent-color system; carry it through the full page.
5. Arrange sections in the order a first-time visitor needs: understand, see, trust, compare, resolve concerns, act.
6. Add only motion that clarifies live behavior or adds gentle energy. Make it pausable and reduced-motion aware.
7. Check narrow screens: stack split layouts, preserve legibility and CTA prominence, prevent clipped product previews, and keep sticky navigation from consuming too much space.
8. Review the page at first load and while scrolling. Confirm that the product is clear above the fold, sections feel distinct but related, and no animation, testimonial, or CTA overwhelms the core message.

## Adaptation boundaries

- Use the reference's composition and principles as inspiration; do not copy Vidyst's logo, exact wording, custom illustrations, testimonial identities, or product-specific interface as if they belong to another product.
- Keep product claims, numbers, compatibility notes, customer quotes, and prices grounded in the user's supplied facts or the project's source of truth. Use clearly labeled placeholders when facts are missing.
- Do not force a white minimalist style onto a product whose existing brand or audience calls for another palette. Preserve the principles of hierarchy, restraint, product evidence, and coherent repetition while adapting the surface style.
- Treat motion as optional. If it adds no useful feedback or context, use a static visual.
